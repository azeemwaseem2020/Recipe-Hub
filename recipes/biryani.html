Chicken Biryani Recipe

There is something special about opening a biryani pot and getting that first wave of saffron-like aroma, mint, fried onion and warm spices. This RecipeHub version is written for a real home kitchen: the rice stays separate, the chicken remains juicy, and the masala is rich enough to flavour every grain without turning the pot heavy.

For the best result, cook the rice only until it is nearly done before layering. The final steam does the rest. This small detail is what gives homemade biryani that beautiful restaurant-style texture.

25 minPrep
50 minCook
4Servings

Ingredients

  • 500 g chicken
  • 2 cups basmati rice
  • 1 large sliced onion
  • 2 chopped tomatoes
  • 1 cup plain yogurt
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • Biryani masala, cumin & chili
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander & mint

How to Make It

  1. Wash and soak the rice for about 20 minutes. Boil it in salted water until the grains are roughly 80% cooked, then drain.
  2. Heat oil and cook the onion until deep golden. Add ginger-garlic paste and chicken, then sauté until the chicken changes colour.
  3. Add tomatoes, yogurt, biryani masala, cumin, chili and salt. Cook until the chicken is tender and the masala looks thick rather than watery.
  4. Spread the cooked rice over the chicken. Scatter mint and coriander on top and cover the pot tightly.
  5. Steam on the lowest heat for about 15 minutes. Rest for 5 minutes, then gently lift from the bottom so the rice and chicken stay in distinct layers.
RecipeHub Unique Idea: Add a small handful of crispy fried onions between the two rice layers rather than only on top. They soften slightly in the steam and create little sweet, savoury pockets throughout the biryani.

Serve It Like Home

Pair it with cool raita, sliced cucumber, lemon and a simple onion salad. Leftovers often taste even better the next day because the rice absorbs the masala overnight. Reheat covered with a spoonful of water so the grains stay soft.

Practical Kitchen Tip

Never stir the rice aggressively after layering. Use a flat spoon and lift gently from the bottom to keep the long grains intact.
